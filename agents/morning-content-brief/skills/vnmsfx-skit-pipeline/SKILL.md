# VNMSFX skit pipeline

This is the compressed house method. It is a starting distillation; Brandon's fuller skills (narrative-tension-engine, cut-dialogue, physical-comedy-pass, shot-list-architect, sound-off-test, storyboard-standard) can replace this file at any time by dropping their SKILL.md files into sibling directories.

## 1. Premise and anchor

A premise is one sentence: who wants what, what gets in the way, and the one move that turns it. A concept with no anchor is cut. The anchor is the specific live thing the skit rides on: a format people are copying this week, a sound, a news beat under 48 hours old, a complaint repeating in comments. Write the anchor as a fact with a date and a link.

Cut rules, applied in order to the candidate list. A premise that fails any rule is out:

1. **Cold open.** The first second is already inside the situation. No setup line, no title card, no "so I was...".
2. **First-line collision.** The first spoken line and the first image disagree in some way: tone, scale, status, expectation. Agreement is dead air.
3. **Zero wink.** Nobody in the skit knows they are in a skit. No glance to camera that says "get it?", no line that explains the joke.
4. **One move.** The skit does exactly one escalation. Two ideas means two skits.
5. **Button.** The last shot lands a turn, not a summary. If the last line could be cut and nothing is lost, cut it and find the real button.
6. **Specificity hook.** At least one detail is so specific that it could not have come from a template: a brand of stapler, a wrong song, a misremembered year.
7. **Share test.** Say in one line who sends this to whom and what the message above it says. If you cannot write that message, the premise is out.

## 2. Cut-dialogue script format

The dialogue is the edit. Write in 15-second shot blocks. Each block has a timestamp range, a visual prompt in one or two sentences that a video generator can act on, and the lines spoken inside that block. Lines are short and rapid; more silence than speech. Three physical beats maximum across the whole script, five to seven spoken lines maximum.

```
[00:00-00:15] VISUAL: <camera, subject, action, setting, light, in generator-ready language>
  CHARACTER: <line>
  CHARACTER: <line>
  (physical beat, if any)

[00:15-00:30] VISUAL: ...
```

Target total length is 30 to 45 seconds. Each block's visual must be readable with sound off.

## 3. The 9-shot camera arc

Every concept gets nine shots in order. The camera tells its own story through position changes, effects escalate across the arc, and colour temperature shifts from the first third to the last. For each shot give: shot number, size and angle, camera move, what is in frame, the FX beat if any, and the light or colour note. Shots 1, 5 and 9 are the anchors: the open, the turn, the button. No two consecutive shots share size and angle.

## 4. Sound-off test

Play the script in your head with the audio muted. For every 15-second block, write one sentence saying what a viewer learns from picture alone. If any block's answer is "nothing new", or the comedy only lands when a line is heard, the concept fails. The fix is never captions. The fix is restructuring: a physical beat, an object, a face, an FX escalation, a visual reversal. Rework until every block passes, or cut the concept. Record the verdict as PASS or CUT with the per-block sentences.

## 5. Captions

Two options per concept. Option A is the deadpan one-liner that extends the skit's world, under 12 words. Option B is the share-test message rewritten as the caption. Neither explains the joke. No hashtags in the caption body; list three to five candidate tags separately.

## 6. Production note

One short paragraph: which generator fits and why (Seedance or Kling for character-driven motion and dialogue sync, Higgsfield for stylised camera moves and effects, live-action phone footage when the joke depends on a real face), what reference material would be needed, and the single hardest shot to pull off.

## 7. Output template for the brief

Write the file exactly in this structure.

```
# VNMSFX Morning Brief — <YYYY-MM-DD>

## Pick one
1. **<Concept title>** — <one-line hook>. Anchor: <what is moving, date>. Sound-off: PASS.
2. ...
(3 to 5 entries, strongest first)

## What moved overnight
- <fact, date, source link> (5 to 8 bullets, newest first)

## Concept 1: <title>
**Premise.** ...
**Anchor.** <fact, date, link>
**Share test.** <who sends it to whom, with what message>
**Script.** <cut-dialogue blocks>
**Shot list.** <9 shots>
**Sound-off.** PASS — <one sentence per block>
**Captions.** A: ... / B: ... Tags: ...
**Production.** ...

## Concept 2: ...

## Cut from today's list
- <premise> — <which rule it failed>

## Research notes
<searches run, dead ends, anything the reader should know about today's sources>
```
